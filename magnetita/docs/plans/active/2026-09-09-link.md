# MAG-P2 — The link, the daemon's second wire and a headless peer

- **Opened:** 2026-09-09
- **Plan ID:** link
- **Status:** active
- **Authorization:** the author said "abre MAG-P2 y empieza" on 2026-09-09
  with `MAG-P1` complete
- **Scope:** magnetita, magnetita-link, magnetitad
- **Implementation checkpoint:** MAG-P2
- **Author-validation checkpoint:** none

## Hypothesis

`magnetita-link` can carry `magnetita-proto` over QUIC with pinned mutual
certificates on one runtime thread inside the thread-based daemon, and a
headless peer built from the same crates can stand in for the phone in every
daemon test.

## Tangible outcome

`magnetitad` accepting and dialling own-protocol sessions next to KDE Connect
ones, publishing them on `org.celestina.Devices1` unchanged, and a
`magnetita-peer` binary that pairs, connects and exercises every capability
from a shell.

## Scope

- `MAG-P2-A` — `magnetita-link`: the device certificate and trust store
  shared with `magnetita-net` (their recipes are the same and move here when
  `MAG-P7` removes the KDE Connect wire), a QUIC endpoint that is both
  server and client on one socket, mutual TLS with the peer's certificate
  pinned after the handshake, the absolute handshake deadline, a control
  stream of length-framed envelopes, transfer streams and datagrams, a pure
  reconnection schedule, and loopback tests: pair by QR, exchange hellos,
  refuse an unpinned certificate, survive a socket rebind.
- `MAG-P2-B` — discovery both ways through Avahi, reusing the daemon's mDNS
  module where the semantics are the same.
- `MAG-P2-C` — the daemon hosts both wires; devices publish unchanged; the
  `Forget` barrier covers the new trust store.
- `MAG-P2-D` — `magnetita-peer`, a headless peer for loopback and LAN tests.

## Exclusions

- Any Kotlin.
- Changing `org.celestina.Devices1` beyond additive `a{sv}` keys.
- Removing anything from `magnetita-net`.

## Build order

1. `MAG-P2-A`, the crate, proven on loopback.
2. `MAG-P2-B`, discovery, proven against Avahi.
3. `MAG-P2-C`, the daemon, proven by producer/consumer tests and
   `scripts/complete-production.sh`.
4. `MAG-P2-D`, the peer, proven over the LAN from another host.

## Implementation exit

The peer pairs with the daemon on loopback and over the LAN from another
host, a Forget revokes it durably, a migration keeps the session, and the KDE
Connect phone still pairs and mounts as before under
`scripts/complete-production.sh`. Met on 2026-09-09 with one amendment the
author ruled: there is no other host, so the LAN run was the peer dialling the
daemon's real interface from the same machine, and the phone of `MAG-P3` is
the other host; the migration stands on the loopback test until the phone's
Wi-Fi toggle in `MAG-P3`.
