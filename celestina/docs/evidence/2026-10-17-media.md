## What was built

- **The duty.** `shell.json` gains `duties.media`, `off` by default. Any value
  other than `"on"` reads as off, and a bad value falls back without losing the
  rest of the file. When it is `on`, the shell listens to MPRIS players on the
  session bus and shows the media piece and page. With it `off` the worker is
  not started. `MediaControl::apply` runs on every adopted `shell.json` read,
  not only when the switch changed, so a worker that failed to start is tried
  again by the next write that changes the file while the duty stays on; a
  worker already running is never restarted by it.
- **Core (`celestina-shell`).** The pure rules: the bounds (`MAX_PLAYERS` 16,
  `MAX_TEXT_BYTES` 256, `clamp_text`), `resolve` (an explicit pin over
  `active`), `active` (playing beats paused, then the most recently heard, and
  a tie breaks on the bus name so the same set of players always picks the
  same one), `cover_url_path`, `identity_from_bus_name`, `now_playing_line` and
  `advanced_position`. The file has no IO.
- **Worker (`celestina/src/media/mpris.rs`).** Two threads on two connections
  of its own on the session bus, none claiming a bus name.
  - *Connections.* The main connection carries the signals and the transport
    commands, each call bounded by `CALL_TIMEOUT` (2 s). A second connection
    carries only the property reads, bounded by `READ_TIMEOUT` (500 ms), because
    zbus bounds every call of a connection alike. Connecting is bounded by
    `CONNECT_TIMEOUT` (2 s).
  - *Match rules.* `NameOwnerChanged` filtered to names under
    `org.mpris.MediaPlayer2`, and every signal at `/org/mpris/MediaPlayer2`.
  - *Signal thread* (`media-signals`). Drains the stream without blocking. A
    signal is kept only when it comes from `/org/mpris/MediaPlayer2`, from a
    sender that is the owner of a tracked player, and with a body of at most
    `MAX_SIGNAL_BODY_BYTES` (256 KiB); the sender and the size are checked on the
    header, before the body is parsed. Pending name changes are capped at 256,
    past which the worker re-reads the bus. A drop guard (`EndOnExit`) marks the
    stream ended however the thread leaves, a panic included, so the media thread
    reconnects instead of following a connection nobody drains.
  - *Bounds on players.* At most 16 players; at most `MAX_NAMES_PER_OWNER` (2)
    per connection, so one process cannot take every slot; the rest are
    ignored, logged once per connection, and picked up by a re-read when a slot
    frees. A `known` set of owners is inserted before the first read of a new
    player, so a signal it sends meanwhile counts.
  - *Quarantine.* A read that times out quarantines the player for
    `QUARANTINE` (30 s), by bus name and by owner (a list of at most 64
    remembered keys beyond the tracked ones, the one ending first dropped), so
    a name that changes hands, comes back, or a second name of the same
    connection is not read early. Signals update the state they carry but never
    lift a quarantine. A player whose read failed or was skipped is flagged
    `unread` and read again once its quarantine ends, at most one such player
    per 100 ms tick; one whose retry fails without a timeout also waits 30 s.
  - *Reconcile.* Every 30 s (`RECONCILE`) the shown player (not every player)
    is read whole as a backstop for a missed signal.
  - *Commands.* The queue holds 8. A command that waited longer than
    `COMMAND_MAX_AGE` (2 s) is dropped, not performed late. While the bus is
    lost, commands that arrive are refused and logged.
  - *Shutdown and failure.* The shutdown flag is looked at between calls and at
    least every 100 ms (`POLL`). A bus that is gone or breaks publishes
    `MediaEvent::Unavailable` and is tried again after `RETRY` (30 s).
- **Covers (`cover.rs`, `covers.rs`).** Only the player the page and the piece
  show (the pinned one, else the active one) is decoded; any other player's url
  is answered "no cover" unopened.
  - *Rate.* At most one decode per bus name per `DECODE_INTERVAL` (2 s),
    trailing: a url that arrives sooner waits, and only the last one waiting is
    decoded when the interval has passed (the worker's `poll` brings it due). A
    cache hit is no decode and waits for nothing.
  - *Pipeline of `cover::load`,* in this order: the URL must be `file://` plus
    an absolute path (`cover_url_path`; the worker already drops an
    `mpris:artUrl` over 4096 bytes whole, `MAX_ART_URL_BYTES`); the file is
    opened through the wallpaper decoder's guard (a FIFO, a device or a
    directory is refused without blocking); it must be a non-empty regular file
    of at most 8 MiB; its first bytes must be a PNG, JPEG or WebP signature; the
    header's dimensions are read on the same descriptor without decoding and
    must be at most 4096 px a side and 16 MP (`MAX_COVER_SIDE`,
    `MAX_COVER_PIXELS`); then the one bounded decoder decodes to 128 by 128 RGBA
    in fill mode, which crops a non-square cover to its centre. Only the decoded
    buffer leaves the module.
  - *Cache.* 8 outcomes keyed by `CoverKey`, the bus name (cut to 256 bytes) and
    the **whole** URL, least recent out first; a player leaving drops its
    entries only. A failure is remembered as "no cover" and logged once, so a
    repeated `PropertiesChanged` does not decode again.
  - *Log.* A refusal is logged with every control character of the chain
    escaped (`CoverError::describe`), since the path is the player's choice.
- **Control (`control.rs`).** `MediaControl` holds the worker while the duty is
  on and what it published: the players, the shown one, an `unavailable` mark
  and one cover per bus name. Turning the duty off hands the join of the worker
  (bounded by 2 s) to `Reaper`, now in `providers::worker` and shared with the
  energy duty, so the loop never blocks on it; the next start waits for that
  reap. Events of a stopped worker are refused. `unavailable` keeps the last
  players and covers but marks them; the next players clear the mark.
- **Loop and Islands.** The Island uploads a cover to the painter once per
  `(bus name, URL)` and keeps the texture across the roughly once-a-second
  players events; a cover for art the player no longer names, or for a stranger,
  is dropped before it costs a texture. If the painter is busy when media state
  arrives, `media_dirty` is set and the whole state is given again on the next
  frame. A new Island draws the media state in force.
- **Pin.** A session-only pin chosen on the page: pressing a player's row pins
  it, pressing the pinned row lets go. The pin is not written to `shell.json`
  and is lost when the shell stops. A name that is not tracked is refused, and
  the pin drops when the pinned player leaves the bus.
- **Piece.** `celestina/src/pieces/media.rs`: a glyph and one line of text,
  drawn only while a player is shown. With the duty off, the bus unavailable or
  no player, the piece is absent. A primary click opens the page
  (`PageId::MEDIA`); a middle click sends `PlayPause`; the context gesture and
  scroll do nothing.
- **Page.** Titled `Medios` (`copy.rs`, `PAGE_MEDIA`), in the 420 by 300 page
  class (`island/state.rs`, `PAGE_SIZE`). It has no tab: the row does not fit
  a tenth one, so, like the Phone page (CEL-2-J), it opens from the piece and
  from `island open media` (`island/pages/tabs.rs`, `TABLESS`). It
  shows the shown player with its cover, the title and the artist, the buttons
  previous, play/pause and next, and, with more than one player, one row per
  player that pins it; a row is keyed by an FNV-1a hash of its bus name, so focus
  and hover stay on the same player when another appears or leaves. With the
  duty off it says so (`MEDIA_DUTY_OFF`); with the bus unavailable it says
  `MEDIA_UNAVAILABLE` and draws no button; with no player, `MEDIA_NOTHING_PLAYING`.
  An open page draws a frame a second by design: its title row carries the
  seconds clock.
- **Verbs.** `island open media` joins `island open activity|notifications|tray`
  in the `Command` surface and in the usage text. The keys keep running
  `playerctl`; the shell does not bind keys for media.
- **Not in the unit.** Seeking. `SetPosition` needs a track id that the page
  does not have, so the progress bar is shown but not draggable.

## Decisions as they ended

- **D1 - active player.** Playing beats paused; among equals, the one heard
  most recently; a tie breaks on the bus name, earlier name first. The rule is
  the core's `active`, and the worker only feeds it.
- **D2 - pin is session-only.** It lives in the worker and in the page, not in
  `shell.json`, so a restart goes back to the automatic choice.
- **D3 - own bus connections, no bus name.** The worker never claims a name, so
  it cannot take a name another player or the shell's neighbours need.
- **D4 - covers are decoded to a fixed square, for the shown player only.** The
  cover pipeline reuses the wallpaper's decoder and its limits instead of a
  second decoder, and adds header limits far below the wallpaper's.
- **D5 - keys stay with `playerctl`.** The page and the piece are the only new
  controls; no key is bound by the shell.
- **D6 - hostile players are bounded per bus name and per owner.** Quarantine,
  the per-owner name cap, the 2 s decode interval and the signal filters exist
  because any process on the session bus can claim an MPRIS name.


The nested smoke (`sh scripts/smoke.sh --binary <target>/debug/celestina`) needs
`examples/fake-mpris-player` built next to the binary
(`cargo build --locked --example fake-mpris-player`); `verify-production.sh`
builds it in release before running the smoke. Its media phase is two nested
runs driven by `scripts/smoke-media.sh`:

- **steady** - a paused fake player with a generated 32 by 32 PNG cover, the
  shell started after it, `island open media`, then `island close` about 0.8 s
  later so the cover has reached the page, and the shell left at rest. It
  asserts the page opened, no `media: cover refused`, `media: cover decoded`
  present, and at most two frames at rest. The page is closed before the rest is
  measured because an open page draws a 1 Hz seconds clock by design.
- **variant** - the same player with an `http://` cover, which must be refused
  (at most twice, allowing the worker's restart), the list of well-known bus
  names identical before and after the player appears, and the duty flipped off
  and on through `shell.json` without a panic.

## Reviews

Three independent reviews, each by a model other than the one that wrote the
code it read.

1. **Opus reviewing Sonnet's code** (the core, the worker, the cover pipeline,
   the page and the piece). Main findings: covers were decoded for every
   player, not only the shown one, so a hostile player could make the shell
   decode arbitrary files; the cover cache key cut the URL; no header check
   preceded the decode; log lines carried the player's path verbatim; a player
   that never answered was read again on every signal. Resolved by decoding the
   shown player only, at most one decode per bus per 2 s (trailing), the full
   URL in `CoverKey`, the pixel pre-check of 4096 px a side and 16 MP before
   the decode, escaping every control character in the log chain, and the
   quarantine described above.
2. **Sonnet reviewing Opus's worker and cover code** (the second pass over
   `mpris.rs`, `covers.rs`, `cover.rs`, `control.rs` and `providers/worker.rs`).
   Main findings: reads and transport commands shared one timeout; a signal
   could lift a quarantine; a quarantine followed only the name, so churn or a
   second name of the same connection bought a read; a failed read was never
   retried; signals from any sender and of any size were parsed; one process
   could take every player slot; a stale command was performed late; a panic of
   the signal thread left the worker following a dead stream; turning the duty
   off joined the worker on the loop and a quick off-on could hold two bus
   connections. Resolved by the second 500 ms read connection, quarantine by
   name and owner that signals never lift, the `unread` flag with one retry per
   tick and early `known` insertion, filtering signals by sender and 256 KiB
   before parsing, at most 2 names per owner, dropping commands older than 2 s,
   the drop guard on the signal thread, and moving `Reaper` into
   `providers::worker` shared with energy (duty off no longer blocks the loop,
   a restart waits for the reap).
3. **Sonnet reviewing the fix pass.** Main findings were on the consequences of
   the fixes: `apply` ran only when the switch changed, so a failed start was
   never retried; a painter that was busy dropped media state; an unavailable
   bus still drew the last players as live; chooser rows were keyed by
   position; the Island re-uploaded a cover on every players event; and the
   smoke measured rest with the page open. Resolved by running `apply` on every
   adopted `shell.json` read, the `media_dirty` re-sync, the `unavailable`
   state (piece hidden, `MEDIA_UNAVAILABLE` on the page), rows keyed by a hash of
   the bus name, one upload per `(bus, URL)`, and the smoke closing the page
   about 0.8 s after opening it before measuring rest, with `media: cover
   decoded` asserted. What this review left open is listed under Open risks.

## Open risks

- **Command target resolved at execution.** A `MediaCommand` does not carry the
  bus name of the player the click was made on; the worker resolves the shown
  player when it performs the command. A click on A within 2 s of B becoming
  shown can therefore act on B. The fix is to carry the shown bus name in
  `MediaCommand`; it is deferred.
- **A failed GPU upload is retried per Players event.** The Island holds no
  texture for a cover whose upload failed, so the next players event (about once
  a second while anything plays) tries to upload it again.
- **Chooser key collisions.** The page keys player rows by a 64-bit FNV-1a hash
  of the bus name. A collision would let two rows share focus and hover state;
  it is irrelevant in practice with at most 16 players.
- **A hostile process can switch its shown name.** The decode limit is per bus
  name, not global: a process holding up to 2 names per owner can make each the
  shown player in turn, so the decodes it provokes are bounded per name, not in
  total.
- **The decoder opens the file a second time.** The cover checks run on one
  descriptor that is closed before the decoder reopens the path. A file swapped
  in between is bounded by the wallpaper limits (128 MiB source, 320 MiB decode
  budget, about 322 MiB peak, see `celestina/src/wallpaper/decode.rs`), not the
  cover's.
- **A stuck FUSE or NFS mount can block the media thread in I/O.** The open,
  the header read and the decode of a cover run on the media thread, not on a
  disposable one; a file on a mount that never answers blocks the worker, and
  the join of the duty gives up on it after 2 s and leaves it behind.
- **A panic in the media thread leaves `MediaControl.running` set.** The control
  keeps a handle to a worker that is gone and does not restart it; the Islands
  keep the last state. Only turning the duty off and on again recovers.
- **An unexplained one-off failure.** One debug-profile run of the smoke failed
  once with "A connection to the bus can't be made". It was not diagnosed and
  did not repeat.
- **A quick off-on of the duty may wait up to 2 s.** The restart waits for the
  previous worker's reap, which is bounded by the 2 s join.
- **Covers without an image extension are refused.** The decoder owns the
  extension rule, so a cover named without `.png`, `.jpg` or `.webp` gets no
  cover. Chromium's temporary covers are affected.
- **`Unavailable` keeps the last covers.** The players and covers are marked,
  the piece is hidden and the page says so, but the textures of the last covers
  stay held until players come back or the duty goes off.
- **Reconcile has no test.** The 30 s reconcile of the shown player
  (`RECONCILE`) has no unit test.

## Rulings and known limitations

- The duty ships `off`; with it off the shell opens no connection to the bus.
- The pin is not persisted.
- Seeking is out of scope.
- The progress is arithmetic (`advanced_position`) floored to the second, so a
  playing track costs one `Players` event a second inside the shell and nothing
  on the bus; a paused or absent player costs none.
- The stream drain and its `ChannelWaker` (about 25 lines) are copied from
  `celestina/src/providers/tray.rs`. The copies are not shared yet.
- The grapheme `ellipsise` in `celestina/src/island/pages/media.rs` is a third
  copy of the same rule, after `activity::truncate_title` and `tray::menu::short`.
